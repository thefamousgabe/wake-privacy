Wake Support

Developer: Gabriel Mckenzie · Contact: thefilthygarments@gmail.com · Privacy Policy

Wake records your runs and lets a ghost run with you on real streets. A ghost can be your past self, your best self, a goal time, or a run a friend sent you. You can also run with no ghost at all.

Need a hand? Email thefilthygarments@gmail.com. Please include your iPhone model and iOS version. If something went wrong during a run, say roughly when it happened. That makes the problem much easier to find.

Getting started

Open the Run tab and tap Start Run. There is no account to create and nothing to set up first. On the same screen, one line chooses who runs with you: Next you, your companion, or No ghost. Wake remembers your choice, and changing it takes one tap.

Common questions

What is a ghost?

A ghost is a saved or planned run that moves along the route with you in real time. Wake shows the distance between you, so you can see at a glance how far ahead or behind you are. A ghost can be one of your past runs, your best self (your fastest split at every point, stitched into one run), a goal finish time, or a run a friend sent you.

What is Next you?

A ghost set a little quicker than your recent runs. As you get quicker, it does too. It is meant to be within reach. However the run ends, it counts in full.

Can a ghost just keep me company?

Yes. Choose your companion and it runs beside you at your own pace. Nothing is scored.

Does pausing ruin my run?

No. Wake never pauses on its own, and you can stop for a light, water or the view whenever you like. Paused time is left out of your moving pace, and the run counts in full.

Will Wake keep recording with the screen off?

Yes. Recording continues with the screen locked or while you use another app. Allowing location access While Using the App is all it needs. If a run ever stops unexpectedly, Wake has been saving it as you go and restores it the next time you open the app.

I don't run outside. Can I still use it?

Yes. On the Run tab, tap On a treadmill? Log an indoor run. Wake times the run and counts your steps and cadence, and you enter the distance from the treadmill display. Indoor runs count toward your totals, your streak and your personal bests. They just don't draw a map.

Can I fix or delete a run?

Yes. Open the run, tap the ⋯ menu and choose Edit run. You can rename it, change the date, correct an indoor distance, or trim GPS drift from the start and end. Delete run is in the same menu.

How do I get my data out?

Open an outdoor run and tap Export GPX. To export all of them at once, tap the gear on the You tab, then Data & privacy, then Export all my runs as GPX. Indoor runs have no route, so they have no GPX file. GPX is a standard file format, so your runs can go wherever you want to keep them.

Why does it ask for Motion & Fitness?

To count steps and cadence, and to offer to start recording when it notices you have started running. You can say no, and everything else still works.

Heart rate zones are empty.

Wake reads heart rate from Apple Health, which usually means the run was recorded while wearing an Apple Watch. If a run has no heart rate samples, Wake hides the card instead of guessing. To check access, open the Health app, tap your picture, then Apps, then Wake, and allow Heart Rate.

Does Wake share my location with anyone?

No. There is no Wake server. Your routes stay on your phone unless you share or export one yourself. On the You tab, open Safety and turn on Hide home zone when sharing. Wake then removes the GPS points near your saved home before a run is shared, so the map doesn't show where you live. See the Privacy Policy.

Is Wake free?

Yes, all of it. This version has nothing to buy and no ads.

How do I delete everything?

Delete the app. All of your data lives on your device, so it goes with the app. To remove a single run, use Delete run in that run's ⋯ menu.

Reporting a bug

Email thefilthygarments@gmail.com with what you expected, what happened instead, and the steps that led there. Anything that affects recording a run comes first. Losing a run is the one thing Wake is built never to do.
