Wake — Support

Developer: Vileking Studio · Contact: thefilthygarments@gmail.com · Privacy Policy

Wake is a running app built around one idea: you race a ghost — your past self, your best self, a pace you're chasing, or a friend's run — on real streets. Every run counts, a pause is never punished, and your effort never evaporates into a failed upload.

Need a hand? Email thefilthygarments@gmail.com. Please include your iPhone model and iOS version, and if something went wrong during a run, roughly when it happened — it makes the problem far easier to find.

Getting started

Open the Run tab and tap Start run. That's the whole requirement — no account, no sign-up, nothing to configure first. The line above the button is where you pick who you're running against: your nemesis, your companion, or nobody at all. "No ghost" is a normal choice and stays one tap away.

Common questions

What is a ghost, exactly?

A ghost is a run rendered as a moving opponent. Wake shows you the live gap — how far ahead or behind you are — and the ghost token moves along the route in real time. Ghosts can come from your own history, from a target finish time, from your best-ever splits at that distance, or from a friend who sent you one.

What is my "nemesis"?

A ghost tuned to be just slightly faster than you've recently been. It gets faster as you do. It's meant to be beatable — the point is a close race, not a beating. Losing to it never costs you anything.

Does pausing ruin my run?

No. Wake has no forced auto-pause, and stopping for a light, water, or the view does not invalidate anything. Paused time is excluded from your moving pace, and that's the end of it.

Will Wake keep recording with the screen off?

Yes. Recording continues with the screen locked or while you're in another app. For this to work, Wake needs location access set to Always (or at minimum While Using the App with the screen on). If a run ever ends unexpectedly, Wake keeps a running mirror of it on disk and restores it the next time you open the app.

I don't run outside. Can I still use it?

Yes — use indoor / treadmill logging from the Run screen. It records time, steps, and cadence, and you type in the distance from the treadmill display. Indoor runs count toward your totals, streak, and personal bests; they simply don't draw a map.

Can I fix or delete a run?

Yes. Open the run, tap the ⋯ menu, and choose Edit. You can rename it, change the date, correct an indoor distance, trim GPS drift from the start and end, or delete it outright.

How do I get my data out?

Any run exports as a standard GPX file from its detail screen, and Settings has an option to export all of them at once. GPX imports into essentially every other running platform. There's no lock-in and nothing to unsubscribe from.

Why does it ask for Motion & Fitness?

For step count and cadence, and to offer to start recording if it notices you've begun running. You can decline; everything else still works.

Heart-rate zones are empty.

Heart rate is read from Apple Health, which generally means the run needs to have been recorded while wearing an Apple Watch. If there are no samples for that run, Wake hides the card rather than inventing numbers. Check that Wake is allowed to read Heart Rate in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices.

Does the app share my location with anyone?

No. There is no Wake server. Routes stay on your phone unless you deliberately share or export one. If you do share a run, Hide home zone trims the GPS points near your saved home first, so a shared map doesn't show where you live. See the Privacy Policy.

Is Wake free?

Yes — all of it. There is no subscription to buy in this version, no ads, no energy timers, and no feature held back until you pay.

How do I delete everything?

Deleting the app removes your data with it, because all of it lives on your device. Individual runs can be deleted from the run's ⋯ menu.

Reporting a bug

Email thefilthygarments@gmail.com with what you expected, what happened instead, and the steps that led there. Bugs that affect recording a run are treated as the highest priority — losing someone's run is the one failure the app is built specifically to prevent.

Wake is made by one person. Replies may take a few days, but every email is read.
